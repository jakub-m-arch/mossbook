A private well-being journal

A journal that stays with you.

Write down your mood, sleep, fears and hard moments in seconds. Mossbook works offline, encrypts every entry on your device and sends nothing to anyone. No account, no ads, no tracking.

Writing

A few taps now, the rest whenever you like.

A quick entry takes two taps at most. Add the details later, or never. You mark your mood with the weather, not with a grade.

Mood
From “very hard” to “very good”, with energy and emotions.
Event
What happened, where, what your body felt and what helped.
Fear
What set it off, what your body felt, and intensity before and after what helped.
Sleep
Hours, quality, waking up at night.
Nightmare
What it was, and a rewritten version with a different ending.
Note
Everything else. With tags and search.

New entries show only their type and time in the list until you open them. If someone glances over your shoulder, they will not read what you wrote.

Voice notes

Sometimes saying it is easier than writing it.

Any entry can have up to five minutes of recording. You record it in the app, it is encrypted like the rest of the journal, and it is never saved as a separate file on the device.

If you like, Mossbook turns the recording into text that you read, correct and later find with search. Recognition runs on your device, in English, Polish, Ukrainian and other European languages. Recordings are not sent anywhere.

Off by default. To turn it on, download the speech recognition model in your browser (487 MB) and pick the file in Settings → Voice notes. The model is NVIDIA Parakeet (CC BY 4.0 licence) and needs about 1.5 GB of memory while it works.

Patterns

Observations, not verdicts.

A mood chart and calendar, plus overviews: how you sleep on better days, which time of day tends to be hardest, what actually brings fear down. Overviews only appear once there is enough to go on.

Mossbook does not diagnose and does not tell you how you feel. It shows what you have written, so it is easier to think about or talk about.

No streaks, points or badges. A break from writing is just a break.

For a hard moment

Help without typing a password.

Your plan for a hard moment, crisis lines and exercises (4-7-8 breathing, 5-4-3-2-1 grounding) are right there on the lock screen. You write the plan yourself, in your own words.

You can add a quick entry from there too. Mossbook saves it, but shows nothing from your journal until you unlock it.

If you see a therapist

A report you put together.

Choose the period, entry types and tags, and Mossbook prepares a clear PDF: your entries day by day and your average mood. You see a preview before anything is saved. You decide what goes in and who gets it.

Your data, your files

Data you can take with you.

When you lock it, at most once a day, Mossbook makes an encrypted backup and keeps the last seven. You can also save a full backup as a single file wherever you like, and export the whole journal as a readable ZIP archive: the entries as JSON and the voice notes as audio files. The format is publicly documented.

Phone and computer

The same journal on every device.

Mossbook still never goes online. Each device writes an encrypted file into a folder that Syncthing carries between your devices: a free, open-source program with no cloud and no account. If you change the same entry in two places, the newer version stays. Deletions, your plan for a hard moment and your settings travel too.

How to turn it on

  1. Install Syncthing on your phone and computer and share one folder between them.
  2. On the device whose password you want to keep, choose that folder in Settings → Backups and sync.
  3. On the other device, choose the same folder and join the journal with its password. The entries from both devices are combined, and the journal it had before goes to its backups.

Privacy

Promises you can check.

Anyone can write “we care about your privacy”. Each of the statements below is backed by code that anyone can read.

  1. 1

    It never goes online.

    There is no networking code in the app. An automatic check on every change rejects libraries that could add it. On Android the app does not even hold the permission to go online, and every release is checked for it. Syncing between your devices is done by a separate program you choose; Mossbook only writes and reads an encrypted file in a folder. The model for turning recordings into text is downloaded by your browser, not by the app.

    One exception concerns building the app, not running it. The speech recognition engine is a set of prebuilt libraries from the sherpa-onnx project, and their build tool can download them. We do not let it: it is given files checked against checksums kept in the repository. No networking code ends up in the app.

  2. 2

    Everything is encrypted.

    Every entry, tag, date and setting. XChaCha20-Poly1305, with the key derived from your password by Argon2id. Nothing on disk can be read.

  3. 3

    Only you hold the key. Not even the author.

    When you create your journal you get a 24-word recovery key. Lose both your password and those words, and nobody can recover your entries. That is what real privacy means, and it is better to know from the start.

  4. 4

    No account, analytics or ads.

    There is no server to sign in to and no crash reports sent in the background. There is no paid version that would change any of this.

  5. 5

    Open code, verifiable releases.

    GPL licence. Every installer comes with a checksum and a provenance attestation that ties it to the public code and the build on GitHub. How to check

What Mossbook cannot protect against

A device with malware or remote access, someone who knows your password, or a glance over your shoulder. The full picture is in the threat model.

Questions

Before you start

What if I forget my password?

You open the journal with your 24-word recovery key and set a new password. Write the words on paper and keep them somewhere safe. In Settings you can check at any time that the words you wrote down are correct. Without the password and without the words, nobody can open the journal.

Does Mossbook replace therapy?

No. It is a tool for writing down and organising your own observations, not a medical device. It does not diagnose, recommend treatment or replace professional help. You can use it alongside therapy, and the PDF report lets you show a professional what you choose.

Where is my data?

In a single encrypted file on your computerphone or computer, with the automatic backups next to it. Nowhere else. Moving to a new device? Save a backup in Settings and open it on the new one. On Android the data stays in the app's private storage and is left out of Google backups and phone-to-phone transfers.

Can I have the same journal on my phone and computer?

Yes. Mossbook never goes online, so it relies on Syncthing, which carries a folder directly between your devices, without a cloud. The folder holds a file encrypted like a backup. Anyone who sees it learns only how many devices there are, how big the file is and when it changed. Once joined, both devices open with the same password and the same 24 words.

When does Mossbook use the microphone?

Only when you start recording a voice note, and only until you stop. The system asks for access on the first recording. The recording stays in the journal, encrypted, and is never saved as a separate file on the device.

Does turning recordings into text send them anywhere?

No. Speech recognition runs on your device (NVIDIA's Parakeet model, CC BY 4.0 licence). You download the model yourself in your browser, and Mossbook accepts only that exact file, checked by its checksum. The text is a suggestion: you read and correct it before saving the entry. It needs about 1.5 GB of memory, so on phones with less than 6 GB it may fail.

Why does my system warn me during installation?

Apple's signature costs 99 dollars a year, and signing for Windows needs a separate process. Mossbook does not have them yet. Instead, the code is open and every release has a checksum and an attestation you can verify yourself. On macOS, Homebrew installs without warnings.

Is there a phone version?

Not yet. Android is next. It will work the same way: offline, without even the permission to go online.Yes, for Android (version 7 and newer). It works the same way as on a computer: offline, without even the permission to go online. It also blocks screenshots and screen recording, and the keyboard does not learn from what you type. There is no iPhone version yet.

Can I open the journal with my fingerprint?

Yes, on Android. Turn it on in Settings after entering your password. The key stays in the phone's secure chip and opens only after a fingerprint check. If someone adds a fingerprint to the phone, the key stops working and Mossbook asks for the password again. The password and the 24 words always work, and on another phone only they open the journal.

Is the author responsible for my data?

Mossbook is provided free of charge and without warranty, to the extent permitted by law (GPL licence). The code is tested and the file format is documented, but bugs cannot be ruled out. So save backups somewhere other than the device as well, and keep the 24 words of your recovery key.

How much does it cost?

Nothing. Mossbook is an open-source project under the GPL. There are no ads, no subscription and no data to sell, because none of it leaves your device.
